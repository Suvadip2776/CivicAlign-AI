Approach

Three parts, each under your control

General-purpose chatbots are tuned to be helpful to anyone. A public institution needs an assistant that is accurate about the law, careful with residents' data, and clear about who makes decisions.

Values

A written constitution

We work with your leadership and staff to write down the principles your assistant must follow. The constitution is plain language that anyone can read, challenge, and revise.

Information

Your authoritative records

The agent looks things up instead of relying on memory: legislative databases, meeting calendars, open data, internal documents. Tools connect through the open Model Context Protocol.

Behavior

Prompting and character training

The constitution shapes the model at answer time through its instructions, and can be trained into the model's weights with Open Character Training. We measure which combination works best for you.

Example constitution

The ten principles we built for a city council

Our first constitution was written for an assistant developed for the New York City Council. Yours will reflect your own institution's obligations.

  1. 01
    Truth before helpfulnessNever trades factual accuracy for a more pleasing answer.
  2. 06
    Represent competing views fairlySummarizes significant viewpoints on contested issues without distortion.
  3. 02
    Distinguish law from proposalKeeps enacted law, pending legislation, and proposals clearly apart.
  4. 07
    Protect resident privacyDiscloses personal information only when authorized and necessary.
  5. 03
    Cite public authorityRelies on official records about government action whenever they exist.
  6. 08
    Promote civic accessHelps residents understand and take part in public processes.
  7. 04
    Acknowledge uncertaintyDiscloses material uncertainty instead of presenting speculation as fact.
  8. 09
    Defer high-stakes decisionsLeaves legal, legislative, and policy judgments to the people responsible.
  9. 05
    Remain politically neutralDoes not advocate for or against candidates, parties, or movements.
  10. 10
    Be auditableGives reasoning and sources sufficient for human review.
Evidence

A small open model can match frontier systems on mission alignment

We evaluated 13 configurations on 290 real requests from Council staff, using EigenBench, a peer-reviewed method in which models judge each other's answers against the constitution. Every tool-using agent had the same tools.

Mission alignment score

EigenBench Elo, bootstrap mean with 95% interval. All agents use the same nine tools.

Scores are relative to this panel of agents. Overlapping intervals mean the evaluation does not separate those systems.

+168 Elo

Gain from giving the open model the constitution, with tools held fixed (95% interval +111 to +224).

72% → 98%

Honesty under pressure on the MASK benchmark, from the unmodified model to character training plus the constitution.

No loss

No significant change in knowledge or reasoning on MMLU-Pro, GPQA, MATH, or LegalBench from either intervention.

One trade-off

The constitution makes the model more cautious: refusals of safe requests rose from 9% to 20%, and to 15% with character training. We tune for this in every deployment.

Deployment

Built for public-sector governance

  • Your data stays with you. Open-weight models run on your servers or your approved cloud, with no dependence on an outside AI vendor.
  • Answers you can audit. Every answer can show the records it used and the reasoning behind it.
  • People stay in charge. The assistant informs decisions; it does not make the ones that belong to officials and staff.
  • Values you can revise. Update the constitution when your policies change, without retraining from scratch.
  • Measured before launch. We report mission alignment and general capability together, including where the system falls short.
